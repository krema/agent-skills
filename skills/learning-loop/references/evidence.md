Reviewed 2026-10-09. This is an engineering adaptation of an existing Copilot workflow-learning package, not an empirical study of continuous learning. Original file fingerprints and the adaptation scope are recorded in [origin.json](../origin.json). No original transcripts or private observations are distributed.

The original package's broader bibliography has not been revalidated for this adaptation and is not presented as fresh evidence. No claim of guaranteed capture, autonomous improvement, or model weight training follows from these instructions. Refresh this design when real trials show mistaken generalization, missed feedback, harmful rule accumulation, or changed host interfaces.

## Conditional recovery guidance — reviewed 2026-10-10

[Sentry v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02994v1), submitted 2026-10-02, accessed 2026-10-10, is earlier research newly assessed in this screening. Sections 4.3 and 5.3 and Appendix A.6.3 compare frozen playbooks with and without persistent exposure while retaining failure-triggered retrieval. Persistent exposure reduced reported performance on the evaluated tasks. The study uses Qwen3.5-9B and GPT-OSS-120B; transfer to other agents and authored skills is untested here.

Engineering adaptation: give reusable recovery rules observable triggers and an exit condition, keep specialized details conditional, and review a success case where the trigger is absent. This supports narrower exposure, not a universal prohibition on persistent instructions or durable preferences. No runtime detector or automatic memory architecture is introduced, and no benchmark improvement is claimed for this skill.

## Rechecking disputed lessons and testing transfer

Lee et al. compared WikiTrace with the same insights alone. Adding linked source records and prompting the agent to consult them improved average scores by 3.2 points across eight suites. The comparison bundles evidence access with prompting. Agents chose when to consult records, so comparing those cases with the rest cannot isolate the effect of consulting a record. Preprint submitted November 10, 2026, accessed November 15. [WikiTrace, sections 4–5 and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14320v1)

Grynets et al. separated migration tasks by target reference to test the effect of reusing answers. Across 118 held-out target groups the paired difference in reference conformity with memory was 0.005 (95% interval −0.003 to 0.017). Conformity measures agreement with a reference. Behavioral correctness required a separate check. Preprint submitted November 12, 2026, accessed November 15. [Migration evaluation, Tables XX–XXI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16143)

The workflow implications are engineering inferences. Revisit authorized evidence when a lesson’s scope is disputed. Distinguish success on the original example from evidence of transfer. Neither experiment evaluated this skill.

## Evidence refresh — November 22, 2026

Search window: November 12–22, with three-day overlap from the previous November 15 screening. Searches covered feedback-derived agent lessons, memory scope and overgeneralization, error propagation, safe persistence/deletion, and official event-capture interfaces. This was a targeted screen, not an exhaustive review. Publication dates and arXiv versions below are distinguished from the November 22 access date.

| Decision | Primary source and appraisal | Limit and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recurrence counts do not establish that a lesson is correct or safe to generalize. Keep scope and source provenance available when reusing lessons. | Jayaram et al., [How Much Should an Agent Remember?](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26361v1), arXiv v1 submitted November 19; accessed November 22. Inspected the methods, AppWorld measurement, findings, deployment examples, and limitations. In its self-distilled-guideline system, support counted how many lessons were merged; a high-support pagination rule still failed because it omitted the API page-size condition. Consolidation also silently lost provenance in two productization paths. | AppWorld's test split was also used to choose settings, most reported configurations were single runs, and the incident came from this system's implementation. It is deployment evidence, not a controlled evaluation of this skill. The existing source-linked, scoped-proposal process already addresses the relevant failure; the operative wording now states explicitly that frequency does not prove correctness. |
| Keep a lesson inside the task and authorization scope that produced it; check applicability before acting in a different context. | Liang et al., [The Memory Trap](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07554v1), arXiv v1 submitted November 5; accessed November 22. Rechecked the version and inspected the full methods, results, and appendices. Across simulated coding, judgment, and safeguard tasks, benign session notes sometimes broadened low-stakes requests or applied them beyond their original context. In one set, 93% of 599 notes were judged broader than the formation instruction; behavioral tests also showed that a wording audit alone did not predict unsafe-action rates consistently. | The scenarios and tested model families are specific, and results do not establish prevalence in ordinary use. The paper supports scope-sensitive application plus behavioral checks; it does not justify treating every preference as unsafe or overriding an explicit durable preference within its stated scope. |
| Do not infer this skill's design from fact-ledger performance. Maintain authorized redaction/deletion and represent missing source coverage honestly. | Islam et al., [LEDGER: Auditable Agent Memory Under Change](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20927v1), arXiv v1 submitted November 16; accessed November 22. Inspected data model, update/conflict/deletion rules, evaluation, results, and threats to validity. It audits both retrieved evidence and answers for stale, conflicting, untrusted, and deleted facts. | This is a fact-centric system whose trust policy and synthetic coding workload were built together; it does not test a downstream agent task. Its five-seed coding results and 210-question LongMemEval subset have stated uncertainty. It informs data-governance questions but does not change the feedback/proposal rules here. |
| Treat event notifications as partial coverage unless the authorized source actually provides the needed conversation records. | OpenAI, [Agents API session webhooks](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agents-api/sessions/webhooks), official documentation accessed November 22; page revision date not stated. The reference lists created, action-required, in-progress, idle, and failed events; it says action details must be retrieved from the session and requires verification of signed webhooks. | This is an Agents API interface, not a local Codex hook or transcript export. It describes lifecycle handling, not a guarantee of complete conversational content. The integration reference now distinguishes this interface and requires explicit coverage reporting; no adapter was implemented or tested. |

Additional counterevidence: Wang et al., [More Memory, Worse Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27406v1), arXiv v1 submitted October 19 and newly appraised November 22, compared trajectory memory, stored abstractions, and on-demand synthesized guidance over three benchmark environments, three backbones, ten update rounds, and three held-out seeds. Stored abstractions did not consistently help; the preferred approach depended on the evaluated setting. This is not evidence to remove durable guidance: the paper studies benchmark agent memories, not user-scoped instructions. It reinforces testing scope and transfer rather than prescribing universal persistence or anti-persistence.

The November 15 checks of [Sentry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02994) and [The Memory Trap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07554) show no newer arXiv versions as of this screen. No official local-host interface change was established from the Codex hook reference. No research here measured this skill's effectiveness or justifies unattended capture.
